## Manuscript-Level Findings

1. The manuscript is strongest when it alternates abstraction with a concrete operating surface. The most effective chain is Ch 6 -> Ch 7 -> Ch 8 and Ch 13 -> Ch 14 -> Ch 15.
2. Core invariants recur productively across scales: operators, transformation, structure, mechanism, and lawful change. This is a strength, but it also creates some repetition pressure.
3. Ch 4, Ch 5, Ch 11, and Ch 16 carry the highest risk of being misread as scaffolding or detour even though they are structurally important.
4. Ch 7 is the heaviest conceptual and mathematical load-bearing point in the manuscript. Its payoff is real, but it remains the sharpest difficulty spike.
5. The late-book run from Ch 12 through Ch 18 is the most cohesive stretch in the manuscript; the argument feels increasingly inevitable there.

## Revision Pressure Points

1. Maintain grounding whenever terminology density rises, especially around manifold, Jacobian, differential, and operator vocabulary.
2. Keep reminding the reader why historical lineage chapters are load-bearing rather than ornamental.
3. Preserve the assembly lens, but continue making its relevance transferable to non-assembly readers.
4. Treat Ch 7 and Ch 18 as slow-reading chapters whose density is acceptable so long as framing remains clear.
