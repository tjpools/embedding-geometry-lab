# Interlude: Shared Linearization

Two people can inhabit the same world and still resolve it through different coordinate systems.

That is the simplest way to understand Michael and Karina.

They do not differ in kind. They differ in basis. Each has learned a different local geometry of the world. Each notices different variables first. Each feels drift under different conditions. Each has been trained by a different distribution of tasks, constraints, and repetitions.

This is why misunderstanding does not always begin in emotion or intention. Sometimes it begins in geometry.

The same situation can present itself differently depending on which coordinates organize it. One person sees lived context, timing, and embodied adjustment. Another sees structure, abstraction, and system constraint. Neither is necessarily wrong. But neither is standing at the same point on the manifold.

When they interact, their meaning systems become coupled. Each becomes part of the other’s state space. Each introduces perturbations the other must absorb, interpret, and respond to.

That interaction can stabilize or diverge.

A small mismatch in assumption can amplify. A shift in tone can bend the trajectory. A hidden constraint can produce curvature that neither person fully sees at first. What appears socially as friction can often be described more precisely as a failure of local alignment.

The goal is not perfect agreement.

The goal is shared linearization.

Coordination happens when two people become sensitive to the same small changes, respond to the same perturbations, and stabilize the same local region of the manifold. They do not need identical histories, identical vocabularies, or identical priorities. They need enough overlap to navigate together without constant divergence.

This is one of the deepest continuities between human and machine meaning. Coherence is not the possession of a final truth. It is the achievement of local stability under change.

Michael and Karina matter only because they make this visible.

They show that human meaning, like every other system in this book, lives on a manifold.
