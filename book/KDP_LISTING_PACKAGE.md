## Long Retail Description

AI is not a mystery. It is a tool you can touch.

Embedding Geometry is a walkable introduction to modern AI for readers who want contact rather than intimidation. It does not ask for a background in machine learning or advanced mathematics. It asks for curiosity, patience, and a willingness to follow structure where it leads.

Across eighteen chapters, Terrence J McLaughlin guides the reader through tools, notation, assembly language, differentials, stories, geometry, and collaboration with a transformer. The point is not to mystify artificial intelligence. The point is to make it legible.

This book treats AI as a built object that can be inspected, tested, and understood. Each chapter offers a clear surface to walk: a concept, a tool, a structural analogy, or a working relation between human and machine reasoning. As the path unfolds, the deeper geometry of the domain begins to show itself.

Readers will find:

- a serious but accessible introduction to how modern AI systems can be understood structurally
- a grounded account of collaboration between a human thinker and a transformer
- a path from programming and notation to embeddings, Jacobians, and reasoning tools
- a way to approach AI without hype, mystification, or empty futurism

This is not a book about magical intelligence. It is a book about representation, structure, and the conditions that make machine reasoning intelligible.

If you want a first serious path into AI that remains human-scaled, conceptually honest, and technically literate, this book is built for you.

## Audience Positioning

Best fit readers:

- technically curious general readers
- programmers entering AI from software practice rather than academic ML
- readers interested in reasoning, tools, and structure
- independent learners who want a conceptual path before specialist depth

Secondary audiences:

- educators looking for an orientation text
- software developers interested in transformer-era thinking tools
- readers of technology, mathematics, and philosophy of computation

This book is not primarily aimed at:

- readers seeking a code-heavy deep learning implementation manual
- readers wanting a survey of AI business use cases
- readers expecting a purely academic mathematics textbook
