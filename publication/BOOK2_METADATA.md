## Short Description

Follow the three journeys that converge in the transformer: artificial intelligence, mathematics, and programming. This inspectable account shows how ideas become representations, operations become executable systems, and components become an architecture whose powers and limits can be tested.

## KDP Description

Transformers did not appear from nowhere. They emerged where three long journeys met: the development of artificial intelligence, the mathematics that made learning expressible, and the programming systems that made those ideas executable.

*Transformers: An Architecture for Geometric Computation* follows those journeys into one working machine. It explains representations, vectors, probability, optimization, attention, memory, runtimes, hardware, interfaces, and testing as parts of a connected architecture rather than isolated technical topics.

No specialist background is required. The point of entry is familiar: every complex system has components, constraints, and interfaces. By keeping those structures visible, Terrence J McLaughlin makes the transformer inspectable without turning it into magic, personality, or metaphor.

This is a book about how mathematical intent becomes computation, how design shapes capability, and why testing, not fluency, establishes trust.

## Back-of-Book Copy

The transformer is not an isolated invention. It is a convergence.

One lineage runs through symbolic systems, statistical learning, neural networks, sequence models, and attention. Another runs through algebra, probability, optimization, tensors, and geometry. A third runs through programming languages, memory, compilers, runtimes, libraries, hardware, and testing.

*Transformers: An Architecture for Geometric Computation* brings those lineages together. It treats the transformer as a working structure assembled from inherited ideas, constrained by design, and made useful through execution.

Readers do not need to begin with machine learning or advanced mathematics. They need only a practical intuition: systems have components, interfaces, operations, and limits. Architecture makes those relationships visible.

The result is a grounded route into modern AI for readers who want to understand the machinery rather than admire the myth.
