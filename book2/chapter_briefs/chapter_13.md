## Reader Entry

Chapters 1–12 completed three lineages: AI supplied the Transformer architecture, mathematics supplied declared numerical structures and transformations, and programming supplied the contracts that make an architecture executable and callable. The reader may still collapse these lineages into one story, mistake shared vocabulary for identity, or treat analogy as a dependency.

## Central Question

At which named interfaces do the completed AI, mathematical, and programming lineages become operational together without becoming equivalent?

## Chapter Claim

The three lineages meet through typed dependency interfaces, not by collapsing their concepts. `ai.transformer` exports an architecture with declared component relations, `math.geometry` exports representations and transformation/comparison rules, and `programming.tools` exports validated implementation and invocation contracts. `convergence.alignment` accepts all three handoffs only when their source modules, exported capabilities, and destination requirements agree. The chapter probe verifies the canonical three-edge alignment and rejects controls based on a missing lineage and a vocabulary-only false equivalence. It does not yet inspect the Transformer at multiple scales, trace full inference, measure limits, or interpret computation philosophically.

## Chapter Result Target

The deterministic fixture should accept exactly three canonical incoming edges, preserve three distinct source lineages, and satisfy every declared destination requirement once. Removing the programming handoff should leave its implementation requirement unsatisfied. Relabeling a mathematical transformation as architectural identity should be rejected even when both records share the word `transform`.
