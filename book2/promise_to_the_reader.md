# Promise to the Reader

Claims about a tool must begin with operations its design enables and evidence demonstrates, not capacities projected onto it by metaphor.

This book treats a transformer as an engineered computational architecture. It operates on numerical representations through parameterized tensor transformations implemented by programs, runtimes, and physical machines. Those mechanisms and their measured limits are this book's evidence.

Fluent output does not by itself establish that the architecture is a person, author, interpreter, semantic agent, or understanding subject. This book also does not turn its failure to establish those predicates into a proof that they are impossible under every account of meaning or interpretation. It is narrower than that, and stays narrower on purpose.

You do not need to begin with differential geometry, machine learning, or systems programming. You already understand the point of entry: a system has components, interfaces, and constraints. Architecture makes complexity inspectable.

Every chapter that follows performs its argument rather than merely asserting it: a claim is paired with an inspectable structure, an executable probe, or a worked derivation, and its limits are stated at the same level of confidence as its conclusions.
