# Book Two Cover — Locked Direction

## Package Text

**Title:** `Transformers: An Architecture for Geometric Computation`  
**Subtitle:** `How AI, Mathematics, and Programming Converge into a Single Tool`  
**Author:** `Terrence J McLaughlin`

Front-cover title treatment:

```text
TRANSFORMERS
An Architecture for Geometric Computation
```

The colon is structural in metadata and prose. It may be omitted visually when the title is split across the two lines above.

## Central Image

The cover is built around a simplified, original rendering of the encoder-decoder transformer architecture shown in the selected reference image.

The image must preserve the architecture's recognizable silhouette:

- input and output embeddings at the foundation
- positional information entering both paths
- a left encoder stack and taller right decoder stack
- repeated attention, residual, normalization, and feed-forward stages
- cross-attention connecting encoder output to the decoder
- a final projection to output probabilities

The cover does not reproduce every internal label. At print and retailer-thumbnail sizes, the visual hierarchy matters more than diagram-level instruction.

Retain only these visible labels inside the architecture:

- `INPUT`
- `ENCODER`
- `ATTENTION`
- `DECODER`
- `OUTPUT`

The detailed, fully labeled technical diagram belongs inside the book, where it can be credited, captioned, and read at sufficient size.

## Composition

The front cover reads from top to bottom:

1. `TRANSFORMERS`
2. `An Architecture for Geometric Computation`
3. the transformer architecture
4. the subtitle
5. `Terrence J McLaughlin`

The architecture occupies the visual center and largest uninterrupted area. It should feel like an elevation drawing of a working structure, not an infographic pasted onto a book.

Use generous negative space around the architecture. Do not place the diagram inside a card, frame, screen, brain, head, or decorative network.

## Visual Language

Direction:

- white or near-white field
- black structural lines
- restrained component colors derived from the reference: pale blue, warm amber, muted yellow, soft green, and quiet rose
- flat color only; no gradients, glow, shadows, or three-dimensional effects
- rounded corners only where they identify transformer blocks
- arrows and residual paths prominent enough to survive reduction
- serious technical typography with no futuristic display effects

The palette is functional. Each color identifies a component family; color is not decoration.

## Thumbnail Test

At approximately 100 pixels wide, the reader must still perceive:

- one shorter stack and one taller stack
- flow rising from inputs toward output
- a visible bridge from encoder to decoder
- the word `TRANSFORMERS`

Internal labels may disappear at that size. The architecture may not collapse into undifferentiated lines.

## Production Rule

The supplied diagram is a reference for structure, not automatically a publication asset. Before any external image is reproduced, record its creator, source, license, and required attribution. The preferred cover artwork is an original redraw whose geometry is adapted for the trim size and remains legible in grayscale.

## Governing Meaning

Book One's cover reveals structure. Book Two's cover displays it.

The cover makes the book's claim without abstraction: the transformer is an architecture assembled from components, flows, repetitions, and interfaces. Its complexity is visible, but it is not inaccessible.
