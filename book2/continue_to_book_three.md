# Continue to Book Three

Book Two ends at a measured boundary.

The transformer has become inspectable as an engineered architecture: representations enter, tensor operations transform them, programs and runtimes execute those operations, decoding policy shapes observable output, and explicit constraints limit what the resulting evidence can support. None of those measurements, alone, settles what an output means, what action it warrants, or who remains responsible for using it.

Book Three, *The Architecture of Geometric Semantics: Berkeley, Transformers, and the Geometry of Inquiry*, begins at that unresolved interface. It brings the technical excursion back to human purpose, interpretation, provenance, judgment, and accountability.

The movement of the trilogy is therefore:

> Book One encounters the tool. Book Two inspects the architecture. Book Three returns the architecture to accountable human inquiry.

Architecture supplies bounded evidence. Philosophy states the assumptions. Interpretation remains accountable.
