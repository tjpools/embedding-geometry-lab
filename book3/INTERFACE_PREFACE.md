# Interface Preface — From Architecture to Inquiry

Book Two and Book Three inspect the same transformer from different explanatory levels.

Book Two asks:

- What structures did the transformer inherit?
- How do its mathematical and programmed components work together?
- How does inference execute?
- Where do measurable architectural limits enter?

Book Three asks:

- Where does operational evidence end?
- Which values and judgments enter when action must continue?
- Who authorizes, acts, answers for consequences, and revises the decision?
- How do derived meaning, closure, and geometry discipline warranted reliance?

The trilogy crosses this interface as an excursion and return. Book Two leaves ordinary explanatory scale long enough to inspect where transformers technically live: representations, learned parameters, mathematical operations, programs, runtimes, and machines. Book Three returns that knowledge to human purpose, judgment, action, and responsibility. The return does not humanize the transformer. A transformer remains an engineered architecture, not a person or biological brain.

The interface is directional. Book Two supplies scoped technical evidence. Book Three inherits that evidence, declares the assumptions added during interpretation, and may return sharper questions. It may not revise the architecture because a different mechanism would make the philosophy more convenient.

The boundary is not a conclusion disguised as a handoff. Book Two does not operationalize meaning, interpretation, or understanding as outputs measured by its probes. Their absence from its evidence therefore supplies neither a positive semantic claim nor a proof of semantic impossibility. Book Three must state the criteria under which geometric computation participates in interpretation, identify who performs that interpretation, and test whether each conclusion follows from the inherited machinery plus declared assumptions.

Berkeley stands at this interface as the historical protagonist. He does not narrate the argument or inhabit the machine. His questions about perception, signs, ideas, and objects create the philosophical pressure under which the transformer becomes newly inspectable.

The collaboration also crosses this interface. The human supplies purpose, selection, judgment, and responsibility. The transformer supplies learned relational capacity, transformations, and alternatives. Tools provide execution and inspection. Testing determines what may be trusted. None of those contributions alone explains the resulting inquiry.

This preface therefore makes one compact promise:

> Architecture supplies bounded evidence. Closure exposes the values and judgment. Humans authorize the action and remain accountable for revision.
