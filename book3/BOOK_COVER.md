## Central Image

The cover places an original line rendering of George Berkeley in structured relation with an open geometric construction.

Berkeley is the historical protagonist and origin of the governing question, not a mascot, authority, narrator, or implied endorser of transformer philosophy. His image must be based on a verified public-domain historical portrait whose creator, date, holding institution, source, rights status, and required credit are recorded before artwork begins.

The portrait must not be synthesized. The final cover uses an original line interpretation produced from the verified source, preserving recognizable historical features without romanticizing or modernizing them.

## Geometric Construction

The structure carries four conceptual states:

```text
perception → sign → relation → interpretation
```

This is a diagram of inquiry, not a diagram of truth. It must remain:

- open rather than terminal
- relational rather than teleological
- inspectable rather than mystical
- geometrically disciplined without implying that meaning is literally spatial

The final path may continue beyond `interpretation` or remain visibly unclosed. It may not terminate in `TRUTH`, certainty, enlightenment, consciousness, or a completed metaphysical symbol.

## Composition

The front cover reads from top to bottom:

1. `THE ARCHITECTURE OF GEOMETRIC SEMANTICS`
2. `Berkeley, Transformers, and the Geometry of Inquiry`
3. Berkeley and the geometric construction on one uninterrupted field
4. `Terrence J McLaughlin`

Berkeley occupies one side of the central field and the construction occupies the other. They are related by alignment, spacing, and directed structure rather than a decorative dividing line or two framed panels.

Berkeley must remain visually distinct from the geometry. Lines may establish inquiry between them, but they may not emerge from his eyes, skull, or body. The composition must not imply mind reading, neural anatomy, supernatural perception, or that Berkeley anticipated transformer architecture.

## Visual Language

The cover belongs to the same trilogy system as Book Two:

- white or near-white field
- black structural lines
- DejaVu Sans or the final trilogy sans-serif
- flat, restrained functional color only
- consistent line weight
- generous negative space
- no gradients, glow, shadows, or ornamental texture

Book Three should be quieter than Book Two. Book Two displays a complete machine; Book Three holds a relation open. Color may distinguish the four states of inquiry, but the portrait remains primarily line and field.

The construction inherits Book Two's visual grammar where appropriate:

- arrows for directional inquiry
- circles for positions or relational points
- dashed lines for constraints or uncertainty
- double lines for enforced boundaries

## Trilogy Movement

- **Book One:** structure is revealed
- **Book Two:** structure becomes a machine
- **Book Three:** structure encounters interpretation

The Book Three cover completes that movement without depicting a conclusion. Interpretation remains an accountable human act performed in contact with structure, lineage, tools, and tests.

## Prohibitions

- mystical or disembodied eyes
- synthetic portraits
- generic AI faces
