# Trilogy Arc — Excursion and Human Return

**Status:** Canonical narrative framing established August 13, 2026
**Trilogy title:** *The Geometry of Meaning*
**Author:** Terrence J McLaughlin
**Evidence class:** authorial reconstruction with inherited technical evidence and declared analogy

## Governing Movement

The trilogy begins at human scale, makes a technical excursion into transformer architecture, and returns that architecture to human purpose, judgment, and responsibility.

1. **Book One — encounter:** the reader meets AI through conversation, tools, building, testing, and reverse engineering.
2. **Book Two — excursion:** the reader follows AI lineage, mathematics, programming, and physical execution to the transformer as an inspectable engineered architecture.
3. **Book Three — return:** the reader brings that architecture back into questions of meaning, interpretation, provenance, collaboration, limits, and responsible use.

The excursion is necessary because a human-scale description cannot substitute for the mechanisms that produce transformer behavior. The return is necessary because technical inspection does not decide what a result means, whether it should be trusted, how it should be used, or who answers for it.

The trilogy therefore answers “What is AI?” through a path rather than a single definition: encounter the tool, inspect the architecture, then interpret its place in human inquiry without confusing capability with personhood.

## Mean and Sigma

The normal distribution supplies an authorial orientation for this movement:

$$
\mu\longrightarrow \mu+\sigma\longrightarrow \mu.
$$

Here $\mu$ names ordinary human scale: limited time, shared language, practical use, and public responsibility. The move toward $\mu+\sigma$ names the costly concentration required for technical study. The return to $\mu$ names translation back into a form that can guide human judgment and action.

This is a narrative coordinate, not a measurement. The books, readers, concepts, and transformer are not asserted to occupy samples from a normal population. One standard deviation is not an empirical estimate of difficulty, intelligence, social distance, value, or wisdom. The mean is not treated as morally superior, statistically optimal, or biologically ordained.

The concise permitted formulation is:

> Book Two makes the technical excursion. Book Three performs the human return.

Any stronger sigma language must identify its evidence class as analogy and survive Chapter 14's self-audit.

The composition is therefore:

$$

The Book One figure is a public glyph for human entry, not a demographic model of all humanity. This sequence may guide interior motifs, but it may not overwrite any locked cover direction.

## Demarcation on Return

The return depends on placing the transformer accurately.

- A transformer is an engineered computational architecture, not a person.
- A transformer is not a biological brain, even when neural terminology records part of its technical lineage.
- Generated fluency does not establish experience, intention, consciousness, authorship, provenance, or truth.
- Rejecting anthropomorphism does not make the system trivial; its learned relational capacity and practical effects still require technical and philosophical inspection.
- The human remains responsible for purpose, selection, verification, interpretation, publication, and use.

These statements constrain the inquiry. They do not settle every philosophical question about mind, intelligence, agency, or meaning.

## Jacobian Boundary

For a differentiable map $f:\mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^m$, the Jacobian at $x$ is

$$
J_f(x)=\left[\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}(x)\right].
$$

It is meaningful only after the map, input coordinates, output coordinates, and evaluation point are specified. A person does not simply “have a Jacobian” as a context-free property. A cognitive model could define a local sensitivity matrix for selected variables, but that matrix would belong to the model and experiment, not serve as a direct measure of the person's intelligence or worth.

Likewise, a transformer Jacobian can be calculated only for a specified mapping, parameters, inputs, and point. Matrix density, sparsity, rank, norm, and conditioning are different properties. None alone means genius, depth, stability, safety, or care.

The conversation's useful intuition is narrower: people differ in knowledge, attention, experience, susceptibility, goals, and interpretation, so transformer outputs do not meet every reader identically. Claims about those differences require human-factors evidence and may not be derived from an invented Jacobian ranking.
