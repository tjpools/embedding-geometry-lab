# Lineage

This crate reconstructs philosophical positions that can clarify, challenge, or limit the thesis of derived meaning.

Modules:

1. `leibniz` — symbolic form, relation, calculus, possible worlds, and rational structure
2. `berkeley` — the historical protagonist: perception, signs, abstraction, and the conditions under which ideas become meaningful
3. `kant` — conditions of possibility, categories, synthesis, and limits on knowledge
4. `modern_geometry` — spaces, invariants, transformations, and the risks of importing mathematical models into ontology
5. `transformers` — the contemporary claim that learned relational structure changes access to inherited language and craft

The historical figures are interlocutors, not authorities recruited to endorse transformer philosophy. Each module must distinguish the figure's documented position from Book Three's later use of it.

Berkeley has narrative priority but not doctrinal authority. His module must establish which questions and arguments are demonstrably his before the rest of the book transforms them. “Ghost of the question,” “meaning is interface,” “the world is in the relation,” and similar formulations are Book Three's language unless primary textual evidence establishes otherwise. They must never be formatted or presented as Berkeley quotations by association.

**Ownership invariants:** C3, P1, P2, P3, R3. Historical voice, scholarly interpretation, Book Three's reconstruction, and contemporary application remain separately attributable under [the ownership contract](../OWNERSHIP_CONTRACT.md).

**Crate question:** Where does the idea of derived meaning come from, and where does that proposed lineage break?
