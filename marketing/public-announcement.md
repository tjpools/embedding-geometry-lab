## Medium Version

I, Terrence J McLaughlin, have written a book called *Embedding Geometry*.

The book asks a simple question: what kind of thing is AI when we stop admiring it from a distance and start touching the system directly?

Its answer is not a trend report, a manifesto, or a sales pitch. It is an orientation book: a first true contact with modern AI through building small things, testing them honestly, and collaborating with a transformer to see what emerges. The path runs from tools, symbols, and assembly language through differential structure, representation, transformers, and the collaborative environments that now shape thought.

The project is also built in layers:

- the book as story
- the epilogue as instance
- the repository as public structure
- the metrics as self-description

So the work is not only something to read. It is something to enter, inspect, and follow.

## Long Version

I, Terrence J McLaughlin, have published a book and public project called *Embedding Geometry*.

At the center of it is a claim: AI becomes easier to understand when we stop treating it as magic, personality, or spectacle and start treating it as structure. This book traces that structure historically and practically, following the lineage from older tools and symbolic systems into computation, geometry, transformers, and collaboration.

The aim is not to flatten the subject into slogans. The aim is to make it walkable.

This is an orientation book, not a technical manual. It does not promise mastery. It promises a truthful first walk: conversation, build, test, reverse engineer, update. That loop is not just part of the subject. It is the working method that shaped the book itself.

That is why the project does not end at the manuscript. The book is the story layer. A short epilogue provides one concrete runtime proof that a simple question can open a deep corridor of understanding. The repository provides the structural layer around the book: builds, notes, artifacts, and executable traces. The metrics provide a self-describing layer: counts, heatmaps, and chapter-level measurements that show how the object tightened and changed under collaboration.

The result is not just a book about AI. It is a public cognitive instrument for readers who want to understand what AI is doing to thought, tools, and collaboration.
